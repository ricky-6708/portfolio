# Orange app store, from smartphone to Android TV

With over 500,000 Orange and partner applications, App Center is an app store helping users to easily customise their Android smartphones by offering the best application to 27 countries across Europe, the Middle East and Africa (MEA) alike.
2020 was going to be a new era for App Center to engage with its French and Polish Orange TV customers, providing a new, immersive and compatible app experience service on Android TV.

## Role & scope

- **Client:** Orange Telecom
- **Platform:** Android TV
- **Role:** UX Designer
- **Stack:** Sketch, Photoshop, Illustrator, Framer, Miro
- **Timeline:** 9 months
- **Squad:** Cross-functional
- **Year:** 2020

![Orange App Center TV hero](/images/appctv-hero-banner.jpg)

## Executive summary

One of the biggest hurdles Orange faced was Google’s ‘Fort Knox-like’ Android TV guidelines. These prohibited 3rd party apps from competing with the Google Store and its corresponding apps. With Google’s input, I managed to circumvent these restrictions by creating two entry points that granted easy access to all App Center apps.

In its first 3 months of launch, App Center TV had gained a total of **7.1m users in France and Poland, of which 79% were returning customers. The TV app has also amassed a total of almost 4 million app downloads.**

## Business challenge

Reaching 21 million users across EU countries, the Orange App Center served as the primary gateway for over 500,000 apps via Android smartphones. To capture missed revenue and leverage the growing smart TV market, which reached an estimated 23 million Android TV units in France and Poland in 2019, Orange needed a new distribution channel to push applications and promotions to its 10.2 million French and Polish TV customers.

### Application comparison analysis

When observing the comparisons between the two platforms, it became evident that smartphones provide a wider range of applications in comparison to TV. Smartphone app use cases include:
- Executing long productivity tasks, (emails, calendar events, messaging, multimedia editing)
- Communicating tasks such as taking photos, videos and reading news.
- Reading articles and blogs.
- Listening to Podcasts.

On the other hand, the TV platform provided fewer choices of applications. These applications provided quick and simple access to immersive experiences for groups to enjoy together.

> “App Center TV application needs to provide instant and simple access via the remote control’s d-pad navigation system.”

> “TV applications are dedicated to audiovisual content services such as sVOD, tVOD, replay, games and education.”

### TV application feature prioritisation workshop

After mapping the App Center on smartphone, I facilitated a remote workshop that included the TV design manager, developers and key stakeholders. The idea was to get everyone to align and vote for the best features that could and should be implemented. The user needs and business goals were also considered. From here, we had the opportunity to evolve the App Center TV application as time went on.

![TV feature prioritisation workshop](/images/appctv-priority-mapping.jpg)

### Point of view statements

Combining the smartphone and TV user behaviours with the feature prioritisation mapping, the team and I decided to define two actionable problem statements. This allowed us to capture the user type and their needs. We also focused on the specific user desires without veering into unwanted feature-focused solutions. The PoVS and the feature prioritisation map were our go-to metrics when creating our ideations and design solutions.

> “A multi-device user navigating via the remote control needs fast, frictionless access to curated SVOD, gaming, and entertainment apps to easily discover relevant content without complex menus.”

> “A busy parent using the television collectively needs simple, direct access to family-friendly educational and streaming applications to provide safe, engaging viewing for children.”

## Ideation

### Google TV entry point restriction

We had that one nagging but huge restriction from Google. Here were their rules when integrating 3rd-party app stores on Android TV...

> “Google states that 3rd party app stores cannot directly conflict with Google Store’s offering. Meaning that all 3rd-party app stores cannot have an entry point that sits on the same level as the Google Store. In addition to this, all apps that reside within the 3rd party app store cannot be positioned above any Google apps on the TV UI.”

![Google Android TV entry-point restrictions](/images/appctv-google-entry.jpg)

### Ideating within the Google boundaries

Despite Google's entry point restrictions, the App Center required high discoverability to clearly showcase Orange and partner promotions. To achieve this, I mapped key integration zones across the Orange TV interface.

![App Center TV entry-point placement ideas](/images/appctv-ideating-entry.jpg)
