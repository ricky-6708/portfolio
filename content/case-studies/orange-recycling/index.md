# A second life for Orange smartphones

Partnering with Orange Telecom, I designed an accessible smartphone diagnostic tool that simplifies device trade-ins for eco-conscious users while targeting a 15% market share increase 
and supporting the group's 2040 net-zero goal.

## Role & scope

- **Client:** Orange Telecom
- **Platform:** Android OS
- **Role:** UX Designer
- **Stack:** Sketch, Photoshop, Illustrator, Framer, Miro
- **Timeline:** 12 weeks
- **Squad:** Cross-functional
- **Year:** 2021

![Orange smartphone recycling diagnostic tool hero](/images/recycle-hero-banner.jpg)

## Executive summary

Partnering with our Beijing development team, I reviewed the initial diagnostic build. While technically sound, it prioritised raw data extraction over user needs. I worked alongside the engineers to redesign the tool around the trustworthy Orange brand, embedding a device optimisation feature and clear trade-in routes. **This collaborative overhaul transformed the app into a reassuring experience, increasing collected used phones from 15% to 28%.**

## Business challenge

Producing a single 120g smartphone requires 70 kg of raw material, over 500 times its weight. With French users upgrading every two years **while 88% of old devices remain functional, Orange aimed to double its collection rate from 15% to 30%**, diverting redundant devices from landfills, recovering vital raw materials, and cutting annual e-waste.

During the interview, I was presented with the APK diagnostic tool built by the development team in Beijing. It provided me with a clear overview of how the tool worked and helped me better understand its use case.

## Define

### How do customers actually use the current Orange smartphone recycling scheme?

The data insights from the research phase showed clear evidence that there was a huge demand for old phones to be given a second life or to be recycled for other uses. However, what was the essence of the whole experience from the user’s perspective? Creating a customer journey map helped the team understand the end-to-end experience the customer goes through to recycle their phone.

![Customer journey for the Orange phone recycling scheme](/images/recycle-customer-journey.jpg)

### Value proposition: pinpointing the opportunities

Mapping the customer journey exposed the gap between our assumptions and how users actually experienced the recycling service. By analysing every stage of the lifecycle, we isolated critical friction points where expectations fell short and used a value proposition framework to define targeted solutions.

![Orange recycling value proposition](/images/recycling-value-proposition.jpg)

> “Strengthen and grow consumer advocacy to trade-in and recycle dormant phones by leveraging the renowned Orange brand. This provides a trustworthy way for users to test the condition of their phones. Greener for the planet and kinder on your wallet!”

### Wait! What? A spanner in the works

A sudden decision was made by the stakeholders to split the design and development into two use cases:
- **Use case 1:** The MVP, where the main function focused on the ability to ‘see the condition of your phone and recycle or trade in your phone in-store.’
- **Use case 2:** To get a valuation of your phone and conveniently recycle or trade in your phone from the comfort of your home.

### Two use cases, but why?

As the launch date was imminently approaching, it became apparent that the infrastructure for use case 2 (provide device valuation, receive payment, packing, shipping options and backend fulfilment) was not possible to implement by this date. 

**Nevertheless, I was tasked to ensure that use case 2 would seamlessly bolt on to use case 1.**

### So how do we progress?

Firstly, I decided to focus on the Diagnostic Tool APK which was presented to me during the stakeholder interview. From my initial assessment, the APK primitively covered use case 1 - **‘See the condition of your phone.’**

However, I wanted to critique the APK to see if it aligned with the user’s behavioural needs unearthed from the customer journey and value proposition map. Does the user have the **Capability, Opportunity and Motivation to perform their desired behaviour(s)?**

## Ideation

### A modular user interface

Before I even began ideating, I had to consider designing a fluid framework that subdivided the base interface into smaller parts. These parts would then allow the design to be modified, rearranged, replaced and reused. Creating this type of framework would enable use case 2 to be bolted onto the existing modular user interface.

![Modular interface concept](/images/recycle-modular.jpg)

With this modular UI framework in place, my next step was to take the value proposition drivers and cull them down into a priorities list before delving into the sketching process. Why? Well, as the deadline was looming, we couldn’t afford to tinker around with endless solutions, so the whole team agreed for me to evolve the following list of features into tangible solutions.

The diagnostic app will enable users to:
- Leverage the Orange brand to build trust and encourage customers to recycle and trade in their old phone(s).
- Run an individual test on each hardware component.
- Run a full test on all hardware components.
- See test results of each hardware component and explain any failures.
- Give a test score after a test has been completed.
- Provide the condition status of the phone: Good, Bad and Poor.
- How to best preserve hardware components on the phone.
- Track usage patterns for each hardware component.

### Sketching

Using the prioritised feature list and insights from the APK, I sketched a broad range of initial concepts, iterating rapidly to refine the concepts. This exploration produced solution sketches that directly balanced user needs with core business objectives.

### User test insights
- **4/5** liked the UX. Some stated they would like to see a solution to the problem.
- **5/5** said it was very important to see the overall score and condition of their phone.
- **4/5** users would diagnose their phone once a month.
- **4/5** would be encouraged to recycle their old phone in-store.
- **5/5** appreciated the fact that they could ‘clean’ their phone. The ability to optimise their phone would encourage repeat engagement with the app.
- **5/5** rated the tool as good to excellent.
- **4/5** said the name ‘Evolution’ was unclear.

## Outcomes

Since its launch, the Diagnostic tool has seen a 13% increase in old phones being collected by Orange.

### Post 3-month insight
- 17%+ Refurbished phone sales
- 8%+ Customer retention
- 544,451 Total users

- 81,000 New users

### Retrospective

Design thinking is rarely a linear path. Encountering an existing engineering build before conducting user research initially disrupted our workflow, but I adapted by integrating the APK directly into our design process to shape a more desirable experience.

A second shift occurred when project scope was split into two distinct phases, restricting my focus solely to the first use case: assessing phone condition. While I initially wanted to design the complete end-to-end valuation and trade-in journey, I adjusted my approach to focus entirely on elevating the MVP. Ultimately, adaptability and pragmatism remain core to delivering successful products under real-world constraints.
