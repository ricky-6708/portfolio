# A new anticipatory home screen for OrangeTV 

OrangeTV provides an over-the-top media service to about 8.5 million subscribers across Europe, including 6.1 million in France alone. With over 20,000 VOD titles to binge. Working with stakeholders, product managers and developers, I redesigned the OrangeTV home screen. Together, we aimed to provide users with a tailored experience, with desired content regularly updated throughout the morning, afternoon, and evening. 

## Role & scope

- **Client:** Orange Telecom
- **Platform:** Android TV
- **Role:** Senior UX Designer
- **Stack:** Sketch, Zeplin, Framer, Adobe Suite
- **Timeline:** 24 months
- **Squad:** Cross-functional
- **Year:** 2017-2018

![OrangeTV anticipatory home screen hero](/images/otv-hero-banner.jpg)

## Executive summary

To eliminate manual searching, I designed an anticipatory home screen UX for OrangeTV that surfaced tailored content based on viewing history and time of day.

- **Reduced browsing time by 43%** (dropping from an average baseline of 17.1 minutes down to 9.7 minutes over 6 months) by eliminating multi-step search paths.
- Streamlined discovery workflows to present relevant titles immediately upon launch.
- **Decreased session abandonments by 17%** (moving from a 35% abandonment rate down to 18% over the 6-month period) through contextual surface-level recommendations.

## Business challenge

OrangeTV users were showing a high level of inactivity during user sessions. In other words, **'users were online but abandoning content'.** Consequently, my goal was to investigate this problem and increase user productivity.

## Discovery

### Stakeholder interviews

I ran targeted stakeholder interviews to establish core requirements and align cross-functional priorities before kickoff.
- Mapped commercial objectives against real user expectations to prevent scope creep.
- Uncovered technical constraints early by auditing existing system architectures.
- Secured cross-team alignment on success metrics and project timelines.

Here were the main objectives and findings from the stakeholder interview…

> 'Encourage users to watch unseen content at the drop of a hat and like it!'

### Not just a functional experience

From a usability perspective, the stakeholders agreed to create a user experience that went beyond just functional. **The design had to be fun, convenient, easy-to-use and meaningful.** Adding this ingredient to the business objective would hopefully produce a VOD service that ticks all boxes.

> 'A fun, convenient, easy-to-use and meaningful user experience'.

![Anderson chart for OrangeTV objectives](/images/otv-anderson-chart.jpg)

Building on the stakeholder insights, **I began to focus on where and why users were browsing but not watching** by compiling existing user data, user surveys and competitor analysis. These research methodologies would hopefully bring to light some interesting findings which could be turned into a tangible and meaningful solution.

### Internal data & usage metrics (6-month average)

I began by churning through all of the existing internal data to uncover any browsing and viewing commonalities amongst OrangeTV customers. I asked the following questions:
- What viewing and browsing behaviours were apparent?
- How did users generally search for titles? How long did users spend browsing?

![Existing internal data metrics](/images/otv-data-existing.jpg)

- **35%** quit after failing to find something to watch
- **18.4%** watched a title based on a friend's referrals
- **16.6 min** average browsing time spent
- **8%** viewed the Recommended by OrangeTV category

It was astonishing to find that **users spent on average 16.6 minutes browsing per session.** No wonder they were left frustrated and fatigued, which ultimately led users (35%) to completely abandon their session. In addition to this, **the ‘Recommended for you’ content only had an 8% user interaction rate per session.** This was an interesting find, especially when considering the fact that this particular row was located in the most prominent position on the home screen.

### Online survey insights with a 1000 participants

I decided to conduct an online survey to gauge further insights. In order to see if the internal data metrics were a true reflection across a larger customer base.

#### Key survey insights

### On-site observational study

Building on the survey data, I set up qualitative usability sessions to observe real-world browsing habits and evaluate how well the existing navigation matched user mental models. I invited key stakeholders to observe these live sessions, using real user friction to build immediate cross-functional buy-in and establish a shared understanding of core UX flaws.

![Observational study stimulus](/images/otv-observation-stimulus.jpg) 

### Usability test: the task

> “Find a movie, series or show that you would like to watch right now.”

Each user was asked to access their personal OrangeTV account so that we could accurately assess their true behavioural patterns when confronting their personal content.
- 8 participants (4 male and 4 female)
- OrangeTV customers

![Lab study observation insights](/images/otv-observation-insights.jpg)

### The lab study results

To unearth common user behaviours, I constructed a framework that entailed the following categories: Success rate, Efficiency, Satisfaction and Attitudes. This framework also enabled the stakeholders to quickly digest where the team should focus its attention to achieve the main goal.

![Lab study results summary](/images/otv-observation-results.jpg)

- **37.5%** success rate in watching the ideal title
- **15.2 mins** average browsing time spent
- **2.8** average satisfaction score 1-7

### Initial commonalities found

Okay, so at this stage, I managed to pinpoint where and why users were constantly browsing and not watching any desirable content. The initial areas of concern were:
- Categorisation and labelling
- Amount of choice - very overwhelming
- Unrelatable content
- Same content on the home screen
- Discoverability of specific content - user's saved, watched content
- Lack of options to search and filter

### How do our competitors do it?

I decided to analyse the attributes of our competitors. Attributes that aligned with our key findings and areas of concern:
- **User preference capture:** How well does the service capture what the user likes to watch?
- **Browsing and navigation:** How clear and consistent is the overall information architecture?
- **Search and filtering:** How easy is it to find the desired film or show?
- **Populated results:** How relevant are the populated result(s) after searching or filtering?
- **Home screen content:** How meaningful is the recommended content on the main screen?

Using consistent, pre-defined attributes across the 4 competitors resulted in a realistic representation of our comparative rankings. This allowed the team to understand what OrangeTV could learn and improve when compared to its competition.

![Competitor analysis comparison matrix](/images/otv-comp-analysis.jpg)
To view the full competitor analysis, [click here](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1gksnXVv0HE-TkniwqT5BcDuYY31viAvAHSD-XtvnC14/edit?usp=sharing).

#### And here’s what we found…

- **Personalised content:** A common theme amongst all of the competitors was that although they provided tailored recommendations and new titles. These titles did not coincide too well with the user’s tastes.
- **Brain freeze:** On certain platforms, the amount of content was overwhelming, which made searching even more anxiety-inducing when titles were not appropriately categorised into understandable themes.
- **Know the user:** Certain platforms requested the user's viewing preferences during the onboarding stage, which provided a customized experience from the get-go.

## Define

### Building our personas

Based on our research, we created 4 persona types that represented the most common OrangeTV customers. This helped us to step out of our own mindset and instead better understand our users' needs, experiences, behaviours and goals. Segmenting our personas into 4 profiles allowed us to continuously consider that 'different people have different needs and expectations when using OrangeTV.'

![OrangeTV customer profiles](/images/otv-customer-profiles.jpg)

### Mapping the personas' feelings and thoughts

Next, I aggregated all of our findings into an experience map. **The aim was to capture key persona feelings and thoughts found during the ‘browsing and deciding’ phase.** It also gave the team a chance to highlight the greatest areas of opportunity and where we needed to focus our attention to create a better customer experience. 

The map also acted as an artefact to build knowledge and consensus amongst the team and stakeholders.

![OrangeTV experience map](/images/otv-experience-map.jpg)

### The point of view statement

Combining the 4 personas with the experience map insights, we were then able to craft a meaningful and actionable Point of View Statement (POVS). The POVS would be our go-to guide when developing goal-oriented ideas…
 
> 'OrangeTV users need convenient and meaningful viewing content because they do not want to waste time endlessly browsing unrelatable titles.’

## Ideation

Firstly, to help us ideate efficiently, we broke up the Point of View Statement into functional and emotional themes. Working within this framework, we also were able to make sure that any future ideas, designs, business goals and user needs did not stray from the main objective.

## Design

### The first critical steps - onboarding flow

I designed a concise and succinct onboarding user flow that was aimed at both new and existing users. Existing users were directed to the personalisation section via a notification whilst new users began the onboarding journey from the very first step.

#### The key purpose of the onboarding feature
- Clearly guide the customer through only the essential steps.
- When a user launches OrangeTV for the first time, the onboarding process reinforces its value and provides instructions that highlight key benefits and features.
- Allow the user to personalise their viewing preferences in order to give them the desired content from the get-go.
- Increases the likelihood of successful adoption or retention.

![Onboarding user flow diagram](/images/otv-onboarding-flow.jpg)

### Wireframing the necessities

I developed a hierarchical menu that provided instant and intuitive access to viewing content and commands using the directional pad on the TV remote control. I began wireframing ideas that focused on the following fundamentals:
- Clear category labelling and visibility.
- Declutter UI and prioritise only the essential content.
- Implement an easy-to-use task flow for the voice and type search functions.
- Quickly surface the content that the user will want to watch.
- Entice users to make quicker decisions when choosing to watch a movie or TV show.

## Outcomes

While the decluttered UI, clear category labels, and hierarchical menu structure drove strong long-term engagement and positive feedback, the 6-month review highlighted ongoing challenges regarding recommendation accuracy during transitional times of day (such as late afternoon into prime-time evening slots), pointing to future refinement opportunities for the algorithmic surfacing engine.

Following the full 6-month post-launch rollout, tracking data across active subscriber cohorts confirmed the following systemic improvements:

> ‘The average success rate in finding and watching the ideal title per user session increased by 23%’

> ‘Abandonment after failing to find something to watch decreased by 27%’

> ‘Average browse time per user session decreased from 16 minutes to 9 minutes - 43%’
