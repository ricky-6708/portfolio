# Self-serve smartphone, targeting €6.75m in call contacts savings

On-device Care Service features that empower Orange customers to independently manage and troubleshoot their smartphones, reducing reliance on customer support.

## Role & scope

- **Client:** Orange Telecom
- **Platform:** Android
- **Role:** UX Designer
- **Stack:** Sketch, Photoshop, Illustrator, Framer, Miro, Jira
- **Timeline:** 6 months
- **Squad:** Cross-functional
- **Year:** 2021

![Orange On-device Care Services hero](/images/osc-hero-banner.jpg)

## Executive summary

I led user research and ideation workshops across European markets to define three core on-device services: **Data Transfer**, an **On-Device Assistant**, and **Screen Share Support.** By translating regional customer insights into actionable product concepts, **I delivered a validated service roadmap targeting a 27% reduction in inbound support calls and a 32% cut in operational costs post-launch.**

## Business challenge

Handling inbound customer support calls cost Orange €5 per interaction, creating heavy financial overhead and unsustainable staffing pressure across regional contact centres. At the same time, hardware sales were dropping due to an outdated software experience that failed to meet modern user expectations. To reverse this decline, **the challenge was to research and ideate an intuitive on-device care ecosystem** that would empower users to manage their devices independently, eliminating future support friction and restoring smartphone engagement.

### The size of the prize

#### Direct call cost savings
Orange handle a baseline of ~10 million support calls annually across 7 European countries:
- A 27% reduction equates to 2.7 million fewer calls.
- At €5 per call, this would deliver a direct saving of €13.5 million over 12 months.

#### Operational cost savings
The total customer service operations budget (including staffing, infrastructure, and call handling) was ~€50 million.
- A 32% organisational cost reduction would yield a total saving of €16 million over the year.

### Validating the six core device services

Six high-priority On-device Care Services (OCS) were pitched to key country stakeholders during a workshop.

#### Voting for the ideal OCSs
The country stakeholders were tasked to ‘add, remove, modify or merge’ the proposed OCSs. These modifications were based on cultural trends and user desires that were apparent in their particular country.

#### The final votes were in...
The country managers and their teams were then tasked with giving a score out of 10 for each of the six services.

![Stakeholder service voting results](/images/ocs-voting-table.jpg)

#### The validated on-device care services
The voting results confirmed that Digital Coaching was deemed the most relevant across all European countries with a score of 43. Followed by Data Security, which accumulated a score of 42, and then came Personal Data with a score of 40.

## Define

The next step was to define the User Needs Statements. Mapping this would allow us to continuously revert back to the user's desires and therefore keep us on track in achieving a functional and desirable product. To do this, we began by affinity mapping the focus group findings into the following categories: Pains, Gains, Anxiety and Habits.

![Customer pains, gains, anxieties, and habits](/images/ocs-pains-gains-diagram.jpg)
![Affinity map of user research](/images/ocs-affinity-map.jpg)

### Problem statements

The team and I created 3 actionable problem statements so that we could determine the common needs.

#### Teen tribes (15-19)
> "Charlotte, a young, active teen, needs to stay online at all times in order to give her a place of value amongst her peers."

#### Tech savvy (20-49)
> "Michael, a multitasking, tech-savvy professional needs to have the best on-device care services to complete and transfer important daily tasks."

#### Easy life (50-75+)
> "Angès, a senior nurse, needs to confidently connect with friends and family to stay in touch with the world."

### User personas

The user needs statements enabled me to develop user personas. We referred to them throughout the entire product development process, ensuring that any proposal or solution would always adhere to the user's desires and pain points.

## Ideation

Now that I had the stakeholder buy-in, the user needs, the personas and the agreed On-device Care Services, I began mapping service flows and ‘how it works’ scenarios for each of the three services.

### 1. Data transfer
Easily take all of your content with you when upgrading to a new device:
- Transition to your new device quickly and securely
- Transfer a variety of data types across Android devices
- Don't lose a thing

![Data Transfer service flow](/images/ocs-service-flow-1.jpg)
![Data Transfer how it works](/images/ocs-how-it-works-1.jpg)

### 2. On-device assistant
**24/7 instant access to helpful information to support troubleshooting device issues:**
- On-device help and support
- No need to call customer services
- Resolve any issues with clear guides and FAQs

![On-device Assistant service flow](/images/ocs-service-flow-2.jpg)
![On-device Assistant how it works](/images/ocs-how-it-works-2.jpg)

### 3.Screen share support
**Get the most from your device with screen-sharing support from your friends and family:**
- Easily share your screen with a secure one-time code
- Quickly get support from your friends or family
- Observe how issues are resolved so that you know for next time

![Screen Share Support service flow](/images/ocs-service-flow-3.jpg)
![Screen Share Support how it works](/images/ocs-how-it-works-3.jpg)

### Workshop crazy 8s and solution sketching

Now that I mapped out the Customer experience for all three services, it enabled me to plan a workshop structured around these lifecycle timelines. Within each timeline, the team would create as many sketched doodles as possible.

I then started to refine the best and most feasible doodles into sketches showing user journeys, interactions, and the UI content. During this phase, I presented the sketches to the product team, including developers, so that these ideas could progress into the design phase.
